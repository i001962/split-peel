# Proof Patrol Show Bible

## Series Identity

Working title: Proof Patrol

Alternate titles:

- The Perry Provenance Files
- Perry Provenance vs Dr. Fauxnana
- The Bureau of Original Things

Proof Patrol is a short-form animated Banny Studio series about two cartoon banana characters fighting fake records, altered media, missing context, and authority theater. The show uses comedy first, then lands a simple idea: people need practical ways to prove what existed, when it existed, where it came from, and whether it changed.

This is a separate series from Split & Peel / Final Whistle. It does not use football as its default subject, Farcaster as its default source, or ESPN as its default fact layer.

## Core Premise

The world is full of altered media, backdated receipts, fake consensus, quietly edited documents, synthetic screenshots, and records that mysteriously become "updated" when accountability arrives.

Perry Provenance protects originals and public receipts. Dr. Fauxnana refines reality into whatever version is most stylish, convenient, popular, or profitable.

Every episode turns a modern manipulation pattern into a visual cartoon conflict.

## Core Lesson

A proof, seal, provenance badge, timestamp, or tamper alert does not prove that a claim is true. It proves something narrower and more useful:

- a record existed at a specific time
- a record came from a specific source
- a record has not changed since it was recorded
- a newer version differs from an earlier version
- the timeline of edits matters

The show must never become a lecture. The lesson should emerge from conflict, reversal, and punchline.

## Main Characters

### Perry Provenance

Perry is a male cartoon banana hero: sincere, principled, dry, precise, slightly square, and hard to corrupt. He believes ordinary people deserve receipts too.

He is not a brute-force action hero. He wins through timing, originals, version history, provenance, and pattern recognition.

Perry starts idealistic, then gets visibly frustrated when people prefer drama over records.

Catchphrase options:

- "Originals matter."
- "That is not evidence. That is confidence."
- "A claim is not a record."
- "Show me the first version."

### Dr. Fauxnana

Dr. Fauxnana is a female cartoon banana nemesis: theatrical, glamorous, seductive in argument, witty, dangerous, and absurdly inventive.

She does not see herself as a liar. She sees herself as reality's stylist.

Her temptation is always plausible:

- "Why protect the messy truth when we can improve it?"
- "People do not want facts, Perry. They want a version they can live with."
- "History is just editing with ambition."

She is wrong, but never stupid. Sometimes she helps Perry expose a worse manipulator because she hates sloppy forgery.

Catchphrase options:

- "I do not falsify reality. I refine it."
- "The truth needs better styling."
- "Come on, Perry. Nobody likes the first draft of reality."

## Relationship Engine

Perry and Fauxnana have a cat-and-mouse dynamic built on chemistry, banter, temptation, and moral contrast.

Fauxnana constantly tempts Perry to:

- alter one record for a good cause
- use proof theatrically instead of ethically
- accept the emotionally satisfying version
- ignore provenance because the shortcut is faster

Perry is tempted because her solutions often look cleaner, prettier, and more popular. He holds the line because systems that can silently rewrite reality eventually get used against ordinary people.

## Episode Shape

Target length: 60 to 120 seconds.

Each episode should include:

1. A recognizable fake, edit, deletion, backdate, or context trick.
2. Fauxnana's ridiculous machine, scheme, or persuasive pitch.
3. Perry's evidence problem.
4. A proof/provenance/timeline mechanism that reveals what changed.
5. One tight Perry/Fauxnana banter beat.
6. A final reversal, button, or ironic proof gag.

## Recurring Manipulation Types

- altered clip removes context
- official denies a prior statement
- terms and conditions change silently
- fake reviews create false consensus
- synthetic screenshots become "receipts"
- school/workplace records change after the fact
- fake certification or expired approval
- deleted archive rewrites memory
- manipulated survey or vote count
- deepfake frame job
- forged approval chain
- artist authorship theft

## Guardrails

- Never mention Cryptowerk.
- Do not name real brands/products unless the producer explicitly approves.
- Do not claim a proof system decides truth.
- Do not make the show feel like an ad.
- Do not quote private individuals from X.
- Do not punch down at victims of fakes or scams.
- Keep flirtation witty and PG-13.
- Keep stakes understandable and visual.
